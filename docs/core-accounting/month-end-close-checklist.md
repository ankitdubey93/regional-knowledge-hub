Month end is where bookkeeping turns into accounting. Day to day, transactions arrive and get coded. At month end you make the books **true at a date**: everything that belongs in the month is in, nothing that does not belong is there, and every balance sheet number can be proved.

The interactive checklist below this article tracks your ticks in this browser. Work through it on a real or practice file.

## The four stages

### 1. Get everything in

Bank feeds, supplier bills, sales invoices, payroll, and card and payment platforms. The most common reason a close goes wrong is a bill sitting in someone's inbox.

### 2. Reconcile

A **reconciliation** proves a ledger balance against something independent:

| Ledger account | Proved against |
|---|---|
| Bank, credit card | The statement closing balance |
| Accounts receivable / payable | The aged receivables / payables report, and supplier statements |
| GST (AU) / VAT (UK) | The GST or VAT report for the period |
| Payroll liabilities | Payroll reports and the amounts actually paid |
| Loans | The lender's statement |
| Fixed assets | The fixed asset register |

Bank reconciliation is the one to master first. Statement balance + deposits not yet on the statement − payments not yet cleared = ledger balance. Every difference is either a timing item you can name or an error.

### 3. Adjust

The month-end journals that the accruals basis needs:

- **Accruals** for costs incurred but not billed.
- **Prepayments** released to expense.
- **Deferred revenue** released as it is earned.
- **Depreciation** from the asset register.
- **Stock** adjusted to the count, written down to NRV where needed.
- **Allowance for credit losses** reviewed.

Example: the March electricity bill (estimated at 330 including tax) arrives in mid-April.

| 31 March | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Light, Power, Heating | 300 | |
| Accrued Expenses | | 300 |

The tax is not claimed on the accrual: GST or VAT is claimed when the tax invoice arrives. On 1 April the journal reverses automatically.

### 4. Review and lock

Read the trial balance as a story. Expense accounts with credit balances, liabilities with debit balances, a bank account that is overdrawn when you know it is not: each one points at a mis-posting. Compare the P&L with last month. Then **lock the period** so nobody edits what has been reported.

## AU and UK differences at month end

| | Australia | United Kingdom |
|---|---|---|
| Sales tax account | GST (one account for collected and paid) | VAT (one control account) |
| Tax return | BAS, monthly or quarterly | VAT return, usually quarterly, filed under MTD |
| Payroll on-costs | Super guarantee, payable with each payday from 1 July 2026 (Payday Super) | Employer NIC, pension auto-enrolment, PAYE paid monthly to HMRC |
| Payroll reporting | Single Touch Payroll each pay run | RTI (FPS) each pay run |
| Construction | Taxable payments annual report (TPAR) | CIS monthly return |

## Common mistakes

- Reconciling the bank account by "clicking OK" on suggested matches without checking them.
- Posting adjustments to the bank account (a bank account only changes when money moves).
- Leaving items in suspense or "Ask my accountant" across month ends.
- Not locking the period, so a later edit changes a figure already sent to the client.
