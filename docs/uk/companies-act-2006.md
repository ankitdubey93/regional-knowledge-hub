The **Companies Act 2006** governs UK companies. **Companies House** keeps the public register. Every UK limited company, however small, must file accounts and a confirmation statement every year.

## Company size

A company's size decides which accounts it may prepare, whether it needs an audit and what it must file. A company is in a size band if it meets **at least two of three** conditions (each "not more than"). For periods beginning on or after 6 April 2025:

| Band | Turnover | Balance sheet total | Average employees |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Micro-entity** | {{fact:uk.ca.microTurnover}} | {{fact:uk.ca.microBalanceSheet}} | {{fact:uk.ca.microEmployees}} |
| **Small** | {{fact:uk.ca.smallTurnover}} | {{fact:uk.ca.smallBalanceSheet}} | {{fact:uk.ca.smallEmployees}} |
| **Medium** | {{fact:uk.ca.mediumTurnover}} | {{fact:uk.ca.mediumBalanceSheet}} | {{fact:uk.ca.mediumEmployees}} |
| **Large** | above medium | | |

*Balance sheet total* means total assets (fixed plus current), before deducting liabilities.

### The two-year rule

In its first financial year a company takes the band its figures meet. After that, its size only changes when it meets (or fails) the conditions for **two consecutive years**. One unusually good year does not make a small company medium. Try it in the **Company size** tool.

Some companies are excluded from the small and micro regimes whatever their size: public companies, banks and insurers, and members of an ineligible group.

## What each size means

| | Micro | Small | Medium | Large |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Framework | FRS 105 (or FRS 102 Section 1A) | FRS 102 Section 1A | FRS 102 in full | FRS 102 or IFRS |
| Audit | Exempt | Usually exempt | Required | Required |
| Directors' report | Not required | Simplified | Required | Required, plus strategic report |
| Profit and loss filed at Companies House | Not required to file (being phased out under new law) | Not required to file (being phased out) | Filed | Filed |

The **Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023** is changing what small and micro companies file: in time, they will have to file a profit and loss account and lose the option to file abridged accounts. Check Companies House guidance for the commencement date that applies to the period you are filing.

**Audit exemption** for a small company is claimed with statements on the balance sheet; shareholders holding 10% or more can still demand an audit.

## Deadlines

| Filing | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Annual accounts (private company) | {{fact:uk.ca.accountsDeadlineMonths}} months after the accounting reference date |
| First accounts | 21 months from incorporation, or 3 months from the ARD if later |
| Confirmation statement | At least once every 12 months, within 14 days of the review date |
| Corporation tax return (CT600) | 12 months after the end of the accounting period (HMRC, not Companies House) |
| Corporation tax payment (small companies) | 9 months and 1 day after the period end |

Late accounts attract automatic penalties, starting at £150 for a private company.

## Statutory registers

A company must keep these registers (at its registered office or on the public register at Companies House):

- **Register of members** (shareholders)
- **Register of directors** and directors' residential addresses
- **Register of secretaries** (if it has one)
- **Register of people with significant control (PSC)**
- **Register of charges** (for charges created before April 2013; later ones are on the public record)

## Persons with significant control

A **PSC** is an individual who, directly or indirectly:

- holds more than {{fact:uk.ca.pscThreshold}} of the shares;
- holds more than {{fact:uk.ca.pscThreshold}} of the voting rights;
- can appoint or remove a majority of the board; or
- otherwise has significant influence or control.

The company must identify its PSCs, keep the PSC register and tell Companies House about changes within 14 days.

### Identity verification

From {{fact:uk.ca.idVerificationStart}}, new directors and PSCs must verify their identity with Companies House (through GOV.UK One Login or an authorised agent) and get a **personal code**. Existing directors supply their code with the company's next confirmation statement; existing PSCs have a 14-day window set by Companies House.

## AU vs UK

| | Australia | United Kingdom |
|---|---|---|
| Who files accounts publicly | Large proprietary and public companies | Every company |
| Size changes | Each year on its own figures | Two consecutive years |
| Annual check-in | ASIC annual review | Confirmation statement |
| Ownership register | Register of members | Members and PSC registers |
| Director ID | Director identification number | Companies House identity verification |

## Common mistakes

- Using the pre-April 2025 thresholds (small turnover £10.2m, balance sheet £5.1m) for a period starting on or after 6 April 2025.
- Measuring the balance sheet total as net assets instead of gross assets.
- Treating a company as medium after one big year.
- Missing the first accounts deadline because it differs from later years.
