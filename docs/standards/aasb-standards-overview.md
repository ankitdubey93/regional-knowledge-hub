The **Australian Accounting Standards Board (AASB)** issues Australian Accounting Standards. For for-profit entities they are IFRS with an Australian number. A company that complies with AASB standards for profit also complies with IFRS.

## The numbering

| AASB | IFRS equivalent | Topic |
|---|---|---|
| AASB 101 | IAS 1 | Presentation of financial statements |
| AASB 102 | IAS 2 | Inventories |
| AASB 107 | IAS 7 | Statement of cash flows |
| AASB 108 | IAS 8 | Accounting policies, changes in estimates and errors |
| AASB 116 | IAS 16 | Property, plant and equipment |
| AASB 15 | IFRS 15 | Revenue from contracts with customers |
| AASB 16 | IFRS 16 | Leases |
| AASB 18 | IFRS 18 | Presentation and disclosure (replaces AASB 101 from 2027) |

The rule: IAS *n* becomes AASB 1*nn* (IAS 2 → AASB 102), IFRS *n* keeps its number (IFRS 16 → AASB 16). Standards with no IFRS equivalent are numbered 1000 and up: **AASB 1058** (income of not-for-profit entities), **AASB 1060** (Simplified Disclosures), **AASB 1059** (service concession arrangements for public sector grantors).

## AASB 101 and AASB 108

The same requirements as IAS 1 and IAS 8: a complete set of financial statements, the current/non-current split, comparatives, and consistent policies. Under AASB 108, a change in **accounting policy** is applied retrospectively (restate the comparatives); a change in **estimate** (a new useful life) is applied prospectively; a prior-period **error** is corrected retrospectively.

## AASB 15 and AASB 1058: revenue and income

For-profit entities apply **AASB 15**, identical to IFRS 15 and its five-step model.

Not-for-profit entities (charities, associations, many public sector bodies) also apply **AASB 1058** for income that is not a contract with a customer:

- If a grant or donation comes with **sufficiently specific performance obligations** in an **enforceable** agreement, it falls under AASB 15: income is recognised as the obligations are met.
- Otherwise, under AASB 1058, the income is generally recognised **when the asset is received** (for example, an unconditional donation is income straight away).
- A grant to buy or build a non-financial asset the entity will control is recognised as the entity meets its obligations to build or buy it.
- Volunteer services may be recognised if they would have been purchased otherwise and can be measured reliably (mandatory for local governments and some public sector entities).

This is the most common Australian-only area in practice, because so many bookkeepers work for associations and charities.

## AASB 16: leases

Identical to IFRS 16. A lessee recognises a right-of-use asset and a lease liability for leases longer than {{fact:intl.ifrs16.shortTermMonths}}, unless the low-value exemption applies. Not-for-profits have a specific option for **concessionary (peppercorn) leases**: they may measure the ROU asset at cost rather than fair value.

## Who must prepare financial statements?

This is set by law (mainly the **Corporations Act 2001**), not by the standards. The standards then say what goes in them.

- **Public companies, disclosing entities and large proprietary companies** prepare and lodge annual financial reports with ASIC. A proprietary company is large if it meets two of three tests, including consolidated revenue of {{fact:au.corps.largePropRevenue}} or more (details on the Corporations Act page).
- **Small proprietary companies** generally do not have to prepare financial reports unless directed by shareholders or ASIC, or if they are controlled by a foreign company.
- Many small businesses prepare accounts only for the tax return and the bank. Those are *special purpose* or tax-basis accounts, not financial statements under the full standards.

## Tier 1 and Tier 2 (Simplified Disclosures)

- **Tier 1**: full Australian Accounting Standards, with all disclosures. Required for publicly accountable entities (listed companies, banks, insurers).
- **Tier 2: Simplified Disclosures (AASB 1060)**: the same recognition and measurement, far fewer disclosures. For for-profit private entities that must lodge general purpose financial statements, it replaced the old option of lodging *special purpose* financial statements.

## AU vs UK

| | Australia | United Kingdom |
|---|---|---|
| Who sets the standards | AASB | FRC |
| Small private companies | Usually no financial report required | Must prepare and file accounts at Companies House every year |
| Simplified framework | Tier 2 Simplified Disclosures (same measurement) | FRS 102 Section 1A or FRS 105 (some measurement differences) |
| Not-for-profit income | AASB 1058 | Charities SORP (FRS 102) |

## Common mistakes

- Treating an unconditional donation to a charity as deferred income.
- Assuming every Pty Ltd must lodge accounts with ASIC (most small proprietary companies do not).
- Thinking AASB and IFRS differ for a for-profit company. They do not, except for some extra Australian disclosures.
