The **Corporations Act 2001** governs Australian companies. The **Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC)** administers it. Whether a company must prepare, audit and lodge financial statements depends mostly on its type and size.

## Company types

| Type | Shares held by | Usual reporting |
|---|---|---|
| **Small proprietary (Pty Ltd)** | At most 50 non-employee shareholders | Usually no financial report required |
| **Large proprietary (Pty Ltd)** | As above | Audited financial report lodged with ASIC |
| **Public company (Ltd)** | The public, may be listed | Financial report, directors' report and audit |
| **Company limited by guarantee** | Members (no shares), usually not-for-profit | Tiered by revenue; many report to the ACNC instead |

## Large or small: the two-of-three test

A proprietary company is **large** for a financial year if it meets **at least two** of these, for the company and the entities it controls:

| Test | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Consolidated revenue | {{fact:au.corps.largePropRevenue}} or more |
| Consolidated gross assets at year end | {{fact:au.corps.largePropAssets}} or more |
| Employees at year end | {{fact:au.corps.largePropEmployees}} or more |

Otherwise it is **small**. The test is applied each year on that year's figures; there is no two-year smoothing rule as in the UK. Part-time employees count as an appropriate fraction of a full-time employee.

## What each must do

**Large proprietary companies** must:

- prepare a financial report and a directors' report under the accounting standards;
- have the financial report audited;
- lodge them with ASIC within {{fact:au.corps.largePropLodgeMonths}} months of year end (on **Form 388**).

**Small proprietary companies** usually do not, unless:

- shareholders with at least 5% of votes direct it;
- ASIC directs it;
- it was controlled by a foreign company during the year (with some relief available).

Some large proprietary companies were "grandfathered" from lodging under old relief; ASIC has been actively reviewing whether they still qualify.

## Every company, every year

Regardless of size, every company must:

- keep **written financial records** that correctly record its transactions and would allow financial statements to be prepared and audited, for **7 years**;
- keep a **register of members** and maintain registered office and principal place of business details;
- receive ASIC's **annual statement** on its review date, check the details, pay the annual review fee, and pass a solvency resolution within two months (or notify ASIC if it cannot);
- update ASIC within 28 days of changes to officeholders, addresses or shares;
- ensure each director has a **director identification number (director ID)**.

## Solvency

Directors have a duty to prevent **insolvent trading**: a company must not incur debts when it cannot pay its debts as and when they fall due. The bookkeeper's cash flow reports and aged payables are often the first signal.

## AU vs UK

| | Australia | United Kingdom |
|---|---|---|
| Small private companies | Usually no accounts lodged | Accounts filed at Companies House every year |
| Size test | 2 of 3, applied each year | 2 of 3, must be met for 2 consecutive years to change size |
| Size directions | "Or more" makes you large | "Not more than" keeps you small |
| Annual filing | ASIC annual review fee and solvency resolution | Confirmation statement every 12 months |
| Beneficial ownership | Register of members (a public beneficial ownership register is proposed) | PSC register, public |

## Common mistakes

- Telling a growing client they "must lodge accounts" because revenue passed the threshold, when only one of the three tests is met.
- Forgetting the test is consolidated: subsidiaries' revenue, assets and staff count.
- Missing the annual review fee: ASIC charges late fees and can deregister the company.
